Few emergencies in a building are as hazardous as sewage backing up into it. Anyone who comes in contact with contaminated material is exposed to parasites, bacteria and viruses, among them giardia, E. coli, salmonella and hepatitis, all carried in raw sewage and all dangerous. Palm Coast Restoration provides emergency sewage cleanup throughout Palm Coast, responding 24/7 with properly trained technicians, appropriate personal protective equipment and EPA-registered disinfectants to decontaminate your property safely.

Water damage comes in categories, and sewage sits in the worst one: Category 3, called black water, the highest level of contamination and danger. Because of it, cleanup follows the toughest rules in the trade, which means full protective gear, barriers to contain the area, tearing out any porous material that met sewage and disinfecting all that is left. Our technicians follow IICRC S500 standards for Category 3 water damage so your property is restored to safe occupancy standards.

The cleanup process involves removing all standing sewage, extracting contaminated water from structural materials, removing and properly disposing of every porous material that contacted sewage (carpet, padding, drywall, insulation), thoroughly disinfecting hard surfaces, applying antimicrobial treatments and completing structural drying to prevent secondary mold growth. Lingering sewage odor is eliminated with thermal fogging and industrial hydroxyl generators, which we run in the affected area.

Why Sewage Cleanup Is Not a DIY Job

Sewage cleanup is not something any homeowner should attempt. Handling raw sewage safely takes specialized training, EPA-registered disinfectants that consumers cannot buy, and personal protective equipment, meaning respirators, Tyvek suits and protective boots and gloves. People exposed to the pathogens in sewage can become seriously ill with skin and respiratory infections, hepatitis or gastrointestinal disease.

Professional sewage cleanup also requires proper disposal of contaminated materials as biohazard waste. It is illegal to throw sewage-contaminated insulation, drywall or carpet in the regular trash, and doing so puts the community and the workers who collect it at risk. Licensed biohazard disposal facilities take this waste from certified restoration firms, which keep the required protocols in place.

Warning Signs for Sewage Cleanup

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • A toilet overflow containing waste that has spread onto flooring or carpet
  • Multiple drains in your home backing up at the same time
  • Slow drains throughout the house that plunging does not fix
  • Lower levels or drain openings giving off a heavy sewage odor, sometimes like rotten eggs
  • Toilets or drains that gurgle whenever you run water somewhere else
  • Soggy, smelly ground over the sewer line or leakage at the cleanout cap
  • Dark, dirty water or sewage rising out of a tub, toilet or floor drain

How to Prevent Sewage Cleanup Problems

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Keep cooking oil, grease and food scraps out of the drain, where they build into clogs
  • Do not wait on a slow drain, which can turn into a full blockage
  • Have roots removed where trees grow by the sewer, before they get inside the pipe and plug it
  • A backwater valve at the sewer connection stops the city system from backing up into your home
  • Route sump pump and gutter water away from the sewer line, which can back up when storm flow overloads it
  • Have your sewer line inspected with a camera every 2 to 3 years to catch potential problems
