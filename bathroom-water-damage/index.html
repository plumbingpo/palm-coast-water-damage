Bathroom Water Damage Help in Palm Coast

Tile, tubs and fixtures are made to get wet on top, but what lies beneath them is not. Once caulk splits, grout thins out or the toilet seal gives up, each shower and flush sends a bit of water through. Hidden from view, it builds up behind the wall tile and down in the subfloor. We help Palm Coast homeowners by tracing the leak path, checking its reach with meters, and drying or removing whatever got wet.

These leaks are usually slow, so the damage is often deeper and older than it appears. Under a toilet, the subfloor may be soft from top to bottom even though nothing looks wrong with the tile. With thermal imaging and moisture meters, we map what is wet before any demolition, and we open just the sections that will not dry in place.

Signs to Look For

  • A hollow sound, movement, or loose tiles in the floor by the bathtub
  • The caulk line at the shower or tub has split, pulled away, or turned black
  • Floor that feels spongy by the vanity or next to the tub
  • Dark grout that never seems to dry out between showers
  • A warped or swollen cabinet floor under the vanity sink
  • Edges of the vinyl floor peeling up, or dark discoloration showing through it
Hidden Risks

What Hides Behind It

A Failed Shower Pan

Every use of the shower sends water beneath its floor when the pan or liner is cracked. Framing under the shower stays saturated even though the surface shows no flaw.

The Room Below

The stain on the lower ceiling can be several feet off from the leak itself, because water travels along pipes and joists first. Its path can cross electrical wiring and ceiling lights.

Mold Behind Tile

Tile and grout are not waterproof on their own. When the layer behind them stays wet, mold grows on the wall board where cleaning never reaches.

Poor Ventilation

Steam lingers after each shower when the exhaust fan is weak or absent. That moisture settles on walls and ceilings, feeds mold, and slows the drying of anything that is already wet.

Why Speed Matters

How the Damage Progresses

The first few days

Water Gets Past

With every shower, the bad caulk or grout lets a small amount of water through. It soaks into the backer board and the edge of the subfloor, with nothing visible yet.

The first several weeks

Subfloor Stays Wet

Showers come too often for the wood beneath the tile to ever dry. Within the wall cavity, and across the bottom of the floor, mold begins.

Months one to three

Signs Show Up

Grout darkens, tiles loosen, and the caulk line turns black. A stain shows on the ceiling of any room below and slowly grows.

Six months or more

The Structure Suffers

Subfloor around the toilet or tub turns soft and joists may begin to rot. The repair now includes carpentry and new tile, not only drying.

What We Do

Our Bathroom Water Damage Process

1

Pinpoint the Leak

One by one we check the supply lines, toilet, shower and tub, then follow the water under the tile and into the walls with meters and a thermal camera.

2

Remove What Is Ruined

Loose tile, rotted subfloor and soft wall board come out. Where the toilet leaked, we treat the area as contaminated and remove porous material that cannot be cleaned.

3

Dry the Hidden Spaces

Using dehumidifiers and air movers, we dry the wall framing, the subfloor and the ceiling cavity underneath. Nothing is sealed back up until we have treated with antimicrobial and the readings show dry.

4

Rebuild and Reseal

After replacing backer board and subfloor, we can arrange tile work, a new seal at the toilet, fresh caulking and ceiling repair, which closes the leak path for real.
